Topic

Shimla and the British

How it began

  • Land taken from Keonthal and Bhajji after the Gorkha war.
  • 1822 — Lt. Charles Pratt Kennedy builds the first permanent house, Kennedy House.
  • 1827 — Lord Amherst, the first Governor-General to visit.
  • 1864 — Lord Lawrence makes Shimla the summer capital of the Government of India; it holds that status until 1939.
  • 1871–1939 also the summer capital of Punjab.

Buildings and dates

  • Christ Church, 1857 — the second oldest church in north India.
  • Gaiety Theatre, 1887.
  • Viceregal Lodge, completed 1888 under Lord Dufferin; now the Indian Institute of Advanced Study.
  • Kalka–Shimla Railway, opened 9 November 1903; 96 km, 103 tunnels, over 800 bridges; UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2008.
  • Rothney Castle — the home of A. O. Hume.

Events that happened here

  • Indian National Congress — planned from Rothney Castle; founded 1885.
  • Simla Deputation, 1906 — the Muslim delegation to Lord Minto that led to separate electorates (held at Shimla).
  • Simla Conference, 25 June – 14 July 1945 — the Wavell Plan; it broke down over the Muslim League's claim to nominate all Muslim members.
  • Cabinet Mission talks, Simla, May 1946.
  • Simla Agreement, 2 July 1972 — Indira Gandhi and Zulfikar Ali Bhutto.
Correction worth noting The Gandhi–Irwin Pact was signed at Delhi on 5 March 1931, not at Shimla — several coaching sources get this wrong. Gandhi did visit Shimla in 1921, 1931, 1939, 1945 and 1946.
