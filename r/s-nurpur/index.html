Nurpur (Dhameri)

Originally Dhameri; renamed Nurpur in Jahangir's reign after Empress Nur Jahan. The Pathania rajas were the most militarily active of the hill chiefs in Mughal service — and the most rebellious.

Raja Jagat Singh helped the Mughal army take Kangra fort in 1620, was rewarded with territory, and then rebelled against Shah Jahan; his fort at Taragarh was reduced after a long campaign. Two centuries later Ram Singh Pathania led the revolt of 1848 against the British — the first armed resistance to British rule in the hills.

  • Brajraj Swami temple — the black marble image of Krishna is said to have been brought from Chittor.
  • Nurpur is a centre of the Basohli-derived painting style, and of Pashmina shawl weaving.
  • Ram Singh Pathania was defeated near Shahpur, betrayed, and transported to Singapore, where he died in 1849.
