The Himachal Economy — Structure

Himachal is a small, high-income, resource-based economy: few people, high per-capita income by Indian standards, and an unusual dependence on three things — horticulture, hydropower and tourism — plus a large public sector and defence remittances.

Structure

  • Services are the largest sector, driven by tourism, transport, trade and public administration.
  • Industry is dominated by pharmaceuticals, cement and hydroelectricity.
  • Agriculture and allied contributes under 10% of GSVA but supports roughly 60% of the workforce — the central structural problem of the state economy, and the reason 'diversification in agriculture and allied activities' is a named syllabus item.
  • Per capita income is among the higher figures for Indian states; HDI is high, with literacy near 83% (2011) and strong health indicators.

Strengths

  • Near-universal household electrification and very high rural road and school coverage.
  • A strong human-development record — Himachal is the standard example of a hill state that converted public spending on schooling and health into outcomes.
  • Hydel revenue and free power royalty from projects.

Constraints

  • Small landholdings on steep slopes; less than 11% of the area is net sown.
  • High per-unit infrastructure cost and a difficult fiscal position — a large committed expenditure on salaries and pensions.
  • Heavy dependence on central transfers; limited manufacturing outside the BBN belt.
  • Climate risk to the apple crop and to hydel generation.
Use current figures GSDP, per-capita income and sector shares change every year. Learn the structure and the direction from here, and take the numbers from the latest HP Economic Survey, which is tabled with the budget in March.
