Topic

Horticulture and the Apple Economy

The single most important economic fact about rural Himachal. Apple is not just a crop here; it restructured land ownership, migration and politics in the upper hills.

  • Satyanand Stokes introduced Red Delicious stock at Kotgarh in about 1916, from Louisiana. Cultivation spread through Shimla, then Kinnaur, Kullu, Mandi and Chamba.
  • Apple occupies roughly 49% of the area under fruit and yields about 85% of fruit production in the state.
  • Himachal is India's second largest apple producer after Jammu & Kashmir.
  • The apple belt runs at roughly 1,500–2,700 m: upper Shimla (Rohru, Kotkhai, Jubbal, Chopal), Kinnaur, Kullu, Mandi (Karsog), Chamba, Sirmaur (Rajgarh — the 'peach bowl').
  • Other fruits by zone: stone fruits (peach, plum, apricot, cherry) in the mid hills; mango, litchi, citrus, guava in the Shivalik zone; dry fruits — almond, chilgoza, walnut in the dry temperate zone.

Policy issues to be able to discuss

  • Marketing — APMC mandis, the role of commission agents, and the shift to universal cartons and sale by weight rather than by box.
  • HPMC (HP Horticultural Produce Marketing and Processing Corporation) — juice concentrate plants at Parwanoo, Jarol and Reckong Peo; the Market Intervention Scheme for unsold fruit.
  • Cold chain and CA stores; the Adani and private procurement debate.
  • Climate stress — declining chilling hours are pushing the apple line higher up the hills and out of the lower orchards. This is the standard 'climate change and the HP economy' answer.
  • Import duty on Washington apples and its effect on domestic prices.
Exam hook Stokes, Kotgarh, c. 1916, Red Delicious. Apple = ~49% of fruit area, ~85% of fruit output. Second after J&K.
